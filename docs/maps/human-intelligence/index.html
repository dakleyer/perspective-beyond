Concepts on this path

  • Attribution GapThe capacity that sustains a result and the capacity credited for it can differ.
  • Audit readinessPrepare process evidence and response capability for a defined review.
  • Auditable provenanceTrack the source, version, timing, transformation and scope of a claim.
  • Avoidable compensatory workPeople move, repair or reconcile information to compensate for a remediable gap.
  • Bounded reference oracleAn evaluator may access fixture information withheld from the system under test.
  • Capability migration and value captureCapability creation, carriers, operation and value capture can occur in different places.
  • Capacity recovery coefficientEstimate the share of released capacity that returns to genuine contribution.
  • Claim statusDistinguish case description, reconstruction, design, hypothesis, test and independent validation.
  • Concurrent commitments of human capacityObligations can compete for the time and skills of the same people.
  • Contribution and recognitionA capability's operational contribution and its organisational recognition can diverge.
  • Contribution evidenceIdentify the technical or operational contribution of a capability.
  • Cost of ClarityProducing decision-ready clarity uses time, effort and operational capacity.
  • Defined and self-assessed maturityA practice can describe maturity through procedures and learning.
  • Discovered valueIdentify useful potential in existing capabilities.
  • Effective response capacityAn actor needs time, understanding and means to act within a usable window.
  • Evidence and maturity ladderSeparate description, proposal, demonstration, test and independent validation.
  • Fallible matched evidence acquisitionRecord coverage, error, cost and provenance when gathering evidence.
  • Fixture label leakagePublic fixture features can accidentally reveal a label reserved for evaluation.
  • Genuine informational contributionPeople produce situated information, knowledge or judgment.
  • Human Intelligence DebtRemediable gaps can require recurring human work to sustain an operation.
  • Human Intelligence Debt measurement unitDeclare the object, population and denominator of the measurement.
  • Human perspective changeReframe an issue so people can perceive additional options.
  • Human workload counterfactualCompare compensatory human work with a technically admissible alternative.
  • Independent upstream outcomeSpecify a service, continuity or capability result independently of local rule success.
  • Internal capability after exitThe recipient can operate, resolve exceptions and learn after the interveners leave.
  • Matched comparisonCompare alternatives under equivalent obligations, information access and budgets.
  • Mechanism ablationRemove a condition in a controlled exercise to inspect its contribution.
  • Necessary execution and oversightWork can be required by responsibility, novelty, law or physical constraints.
  • Observable maturityRepeated practice and learning provide evidence of maturity.
  • Organisational attributionDecision-makers assign recognition and credit for capability.
  • Outcome level and boundaryLocate a performance claim at task, team, organisation, network or programme level.
  • Partial order of non-fungible dimensionsCompare measures with declared actors, units and directions.
  • Repeatable internal learningTraining, codification and practice can make an intervention reproducible.
  • Reusable capabilityA useful function can sometimes outlive the tool or channel where it first appeared.
  • Revaluation before clarificationReconstruct contribution, dependencies, substitutes and tacit knowledge before deciding continuity.
  • Savings from qualified reuseReuse can save effort when scope, permissions and validity are checked.
  • Three costs of producing clarityAccount for discovery, authorised constitution and operational absorption.
  • Total intervention costCount discovery, activation, execution, governance, disruption, learning and closure within the boundary.
  • Traceable explanationReconstruct why a recommendation or decision was produced.
  • Transmission of tacit judgmentPeople learn to recognise exceptions through situated practice.
  • Usable human capabilityPeople must be able to perceive and exercise an option within the operating architecture.
